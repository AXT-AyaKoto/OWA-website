## Q. Who are you?

A. We are a group of **people who create websites, mobile apps, and similar things.**  

For more about our members, see [here](https://open-web-advocacy.org/ja/contributors/).

> Draft Author's Note:
> - *people who create websites, mobile apps, and similar things.*
>     - I wanted to use "software engineers" or "programmers", but thought many might not relate to those terms, so I chose a descriptive phrase.
> - *Members of the group*
>     - Refers to "contributors" or "supporters", but neither term feels very familiar in Japanese.

## Q. What is your goal?

A. Our goal is to **protect the future of app development** by promoting an **"open web".**

> Draft Author's Note:
> - The existing description was ambiguous about whether it's about the future of the web or app development.
>     - I interpret it as "promoting the open web in order to protect the future of app development". Is this correct? If not, this whole draft needs revision.
> - *"open web"*
>     - I struggled to come up with a simpler way to express this. Suggestions welcome.

We believe that the future of app development is in danger.
Big companies like Apple and Google (which we call **"Big Tech"**) charge us high fees.  
But it doesn't stop there. Big Tech is trying to monopolize the app ecosystem.  
If they succeed, it will become harder for new ideas to emerge.  
That’s why we are fighting against Big Tech.

> Draft Author's Note: Here is the original text for reference:
> > The future of app development is at risk.  
> > Large companies like Apple and Google impose heavy "taxes" on anyone who wants to publish an app.  
> > On top of that, they are desperately trying to take away our freedom to monopolize the market.  
> > If they succeed, app development will lose competition, and innovation will suffer.  
> > To prevent that, we are standing up to them.

> Draft Author's Note:
> - *Big companies like Apple and Google (called "Big Tech")*
>     - It’s important to introduce a word for "Big Tech" here to keep the text readable.
>     - Since "gatekeeper" isn't common in Japanese, I chose "Big Tech" as a familiar alternative.
>     - In English translation, using "gatekeepers" might actually be better.
> - *Harder for new ideas to emerge*
>     - Saying "competition breaks down" wouldn’t be understood by most people. Those who do understand would get the implications anyway.
